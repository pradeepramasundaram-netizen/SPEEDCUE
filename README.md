# SPEEDCUE
SPEEDCUE

Road accidents involving pedestrians, passenger vehicles, and heavy goods carriers are significantly influenced by the inability of road users to intuitively estimate the speed and approaching risk of an oncoming vehicle, particularly in low-visibility and careless pedestrians. Pedestrians may make unsafe crossing decisions when the distance of a vehicle appears sufficient but its actual speed is underestimated. This risk becomes more critical for heavy commercial vehicles carrying substantial loads, where sudden emergency braking can result in cargo displacement, loss of vehicle stability, increased stopping distance, or cause accidents. In addition, the widespread use of high-intensity white LED headlights at night can produce excessive glare, particularly in urban environments, temporarily reducing the visual comfort and visibility of oncoming drivers and pedestrians.
To address these interconnected challenges, this project proposes an Intelligent Speed Warning Headlight System that uses vehicle speed as a primary control parameter for automatically changing the headlight illumination mode. During low-speed and controlled-speed operation, such as urban roads, pedestrian zones, intersections, and restricted zones, the system operates in a warm-yellow illumination mode. At higher speeds, particularly on highways and open roads, it automatically transitions to bright-white LED illumination to provide enhanced forward visibility. The color transition is designed to function as an intuitive and easily recognizable visual speed cue, enabling pedestrians and other road users to better perceive the relative kinetic threat of an approaching vehicle without requiring numerical speed information.
The proposed Smart Speed-Indicating Headlight System addresses these challenges by introducing a dynamic headlight mechanism that simultaneously provides road illumination and acts as a visual speed indicator. The system automatically changes the headlight illumination characteristics according to the vehicle's operating speed. During low-speed operation, such as in urban roads, pedestrian zones, restricted zones, and residential environments, the system activates a warm-yellow halogen-based illumination mode. At higher speeds, particularly on highways and open roads, it transitions to a bright-white LED illumination mode. The change in headlight color provides an intuitive and easily recognizable visual cue to pedestrians and surrounding road users regarding the vehicle's speed condition, without requiring them to estimate speed numerically.
The proposed implementation will progress through simulation and algorithm development, hardware prototyping, controlled road-environment testing, glare and visibility evaluation, threshold optimization. Performance will be evaluated using measurable parameters including headlight transition response time, glare reduction, visibility distance, pedestrian speed-perception accuracy, system reliability, and potential reduction in unsafe crossing or delayed-reaction events. The project supports SDG 9 – Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure by promoting innovation in intelligent vehicle-lighting technology and developing a low-cost, scalable road-safety solution. The proposed concept offers a not existing application of speed-responsive headlight illumination as a visual communication interface.
The overall objective is to develop a scalable, low-cost, and practically integrable vehicle lighting technology that transforms the headlight from a passive illumination device into an active visual communication and road-safety interface for pedestrians, drivers, and heavy goods vehicle operators.
